# I made a gummy alligator fight. Now I can't tell how hard it is.

Hey folks! I've been working on Gummy Gator, and I wanted to get a little of that Punch-Out!! feeling into DDDumpling. Watch the opponent, spot the tell, defend, then get your hits in before the opening closes. Except the opponent is a large gummy alligator. 🐊

I also wanted the companion to actually take part in the fight. The attacks are coming for your little friend, so watching them matters. I'm using visual indicators to help you read the incoming attacks and figure out when to defend and when to hit back, while keeping the action on the playfield.

Swipe across a glowing arm or tail to parry. Complete the defense and there's a short opening to hit back with the keys. Get a clean sequence going and you can earn a longer opening. I'm trying to make that exchange feel readable and satisfying, with the companion right in the middle of it.

Getting the difficulty right has been rough! The fight was VERY hard at first. I've played it so many times while tuning it that I expect I'm now completely blind to how difficult it actually is. I know what's coming. A new player doesn't. Something that feels obvious to me might just look like a gator beating up their friend.

In the development build, Boss Time Attack makes repeated attempts much easier to test. I can replay the fight without another Adventure run, and the results screen records the time, defenses and damage taken.

I've attached three short clips with game music and sound effects: parrying into an opening, handling the later combos, and finishing the fight. The GIFs below are silent previews.

That tells me the fight works; it doesn't tell me whether a new player can read it. This post previews work in progress and does not announce Gator as available in the current public downloads.

DDDumpling is free, offline, and has no ads or payments. The project is AI-assisted, including code, graphics, sounds and writing; I'm doing the design, playtesting and tuning.

I'd love some fresh eyes on the clips: did you spot the incoming attack, did the parry make sense, and was it clear when the player could hit back? If something only made sense on a second viewing, that's exactly the bit I want to hear about.
